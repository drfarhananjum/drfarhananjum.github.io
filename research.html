Seeing cells one molecule at a time

Six themes that connect imaging methods, fluorescent probes and cell biology.

Research agenda

Cells hold structure that ordinary light microscopes cannot resolve. My work brings organelles, proteins and DNA into view at the nanometre scale, and builds the probes that make this possible. I then use those tools to ask how organelles change during mitophagy, after mutation and under drug treatment in cancer cells.

At IIT Hyderabad I prepare samples and acquire images on single-molecule and super-resolution systems. I am glad to talk about collaborations on imaging, probe design or cell biology.

Facility work and method

Fluorescent nanoprobes for organelles

Probe design

Carbon nanodots, silver nanoclusters, quantum dots, iron oxide particles and metal complexes that stain mitochondria, lysosomes, the nucleolus and DNA. The probes are bright and photostable, work with super-resolution methods, and include near-infrared emitters for imaging in living animals such as C. elegans.

BSA-dye bioconjugate probe

Indian patent, granted

A simple way to join NHS-ester dyes (Atto and Cyanine) to bovine serum albumin. The conjugates are stable and biocompatible lysosome probes with controlled blinking and steady emission, which suits STORM and PALM. Developed with Prof. Chayan Kanti Nandi and colleagues at IIT Mandi.

Cancer cells, chromatin and DNA damage

Nanomedicine and epigenetics

Fluorescence lifetime imaging and super-resolution microscopy map how DNA breaks down after doxorubicin on PEGylated carbogenic nanodots. A newer line of work images histone marks H3K4me3 and H3K9me3 in triple-negative breast cancer cell lines, and chromatin reorganisation in plants.

Organelle structure under change

Cell biology

Super-resolution images show mitochondria shrinking and lysosomes expanding during mitophagy, and how organelle structure shifts together after a point mutation.
